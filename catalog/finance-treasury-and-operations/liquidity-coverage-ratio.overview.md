# Liquidity Coverage Ratio Overview

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is a binding regulatory short-term liquidity standard and a crucial operational metric that assesses an institution's financial resilience against liquidity disruptions. It is designed to ensure that financial institutions maintain sufficient high-quality liquid assets to cover net cash outflows over a 30-day stress scenario.

## Definition and Purpose

The LCR is a regulatory and operational metric evaluating short-term resilience against liquidity disruptions. It assesses a firm's ability to withstand a specific stressed funding scenario lasting 30 calendar days, ensuring it has enough high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to offset potential net cash outflows.

## Formula

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio is calculated using the following formula:

$$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA)}}{\text{Total Net Cash Outflows over a 30-day stress scenario}}$$

This is also expressed as:

$$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA)}}{\text{30-day Net Cash Outflows}}$$

## Regulatory and Operational Framework

The LCR operates under the Basel III regulatory framework, which sets specific thresholds and rules.

*   **Regulatory Minimum:** Basel III rules mandate a strict LCR minimum floor of $\ge 100\%$. Any breach of this minimum, even an intraday one, is considered a notifiable compliance event requiring immediate escalation.
*   **Internal Management Target:** To provide an operating buffer and ensure normal business fluctuations do not approach the regulatory floor, internal risk appetite targets are typically set to an operational buffer of approximately $110\%$. This target is steered daily by adjusting the HQLA portfolio.
