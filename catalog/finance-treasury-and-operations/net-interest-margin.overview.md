# Net Interest Margin Overview

Net Interest Margin (NIM), also known as nim, is a core bank profitability metric. It is defined as net interest income divided by average interest-earning assets, signaling how much margin the balance sheet produces per dollar of earning assets. This metric serves as the core measure of balance-sheet profitability.

## Metadata Definition

Net Interest Margin is a structural metric that quantifies the profitability derived from a bank's interest-earning assets.

## Formula

The formula for Net Interest Margin is:

$$\text{NIM} = \frac{\text{Interest Income} - \text{Interest Expense}}{\text{Average Earning Assets}}$$

This formula essentially calculates net interest income (Interest Income minus Interest Expense) and divides it by the Average Earning Assets.

## Behavioral Rule

NIM's behavior is directly tied to the relationship between funding costs and asset yields:
*   NIM compresses when funding costs rise faster than asset yields.
*   NIM expands when asset yields outpace funding costs.
