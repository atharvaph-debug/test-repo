# Customer Lifetime Value Overview

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), also referred to as Customer LTV or simply LTV, represents the expected net profit generated over the entire duration of a customer relationship. This crucial financial metric is utilized by both marketing and finance teams.

## Key Definitions and Aliases

The Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) is defined as the projected revenue from a customer relationship, minus the associated cost-to-serve and retention spend. While the finance team and official dashboards typically publish this metric as "CLV", growth marketing decks often label the identical metric as "Customer LTV" or "LTV". Recognizing these as the same structural metadata concept is critical for ensuring accurate cross-functional reporting and optimizing campaign spend.

## Purpose and Application

CLV is a key metric for strategic decision-making. Marketing and finance teams use it for cross-functional reporting and campaign spend optimization. It also plays a significant role in customer retention strategies. For instance, when addressing subscriber churn, identifying and protecting high-value customer lifetime value segments, such as out-of-contract, single-product subscribers, is a priority for retention efforts. Frontline care agents are specifically directed to consider CLV when applying approved retention options before processing cancellations.
