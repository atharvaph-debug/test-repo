# Suspicious Activity Report Overview

A Suspicious Activity Report (SAR), also known by its alias `sar`, is a formal, time-sensitive regulatory filing submitted when an investigator establishes a reasonable suspicion of illicit transaction activity. It represents the formal, time-bound endpoint of the compliance chain in financial crime prevention.

## Key Features

SARs are critical for regulatory compliance and financial crime prevention. They are filed when an investigator determines there is reasonable suspicion of illicit conduct, typically following an investigation triggered by transaction monitoring alerts. These alerts are generated when active transaction metadata deviates from baseline behaviors established in a customer's KYC (Know Your Customer) profile, such as unusual transaction volumes, structuring of funds, or unexpected movement of funds.

Key metadata constraints associated with a SAR include:
*   A **strict, confidential regulatory filing window** that requires "no tipping off" the subject of the report.
*   A **well-documented narrative** that explicitly connects observed transaction patterns directly to the suspicion of illicit activity.

The filing of a SAR is a crucial step in addressing potential money laundering and other financial crimes.
