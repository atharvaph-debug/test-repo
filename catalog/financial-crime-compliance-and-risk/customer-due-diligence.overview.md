# Customer Due Diligence Overview

Customer Due Diligence (CDD) refers to the process of verifying applicant identities and beneficial owners during customer onboarding, along with subsequent periodic profile refreshes. In policy documents, Customer Due Diligence (CDD) is treated as an equivalent term to Know Your Customer (KYC).

## Key Features and Concepts

*   **Identity Verification:** CDD (or KYC) involves verifying the identities of applicants, including beneficial owners for non-individual entities, during the initial onboarding process.
*   **Profile Refresh:** The process also includes periodically refreshing customer profiles to ensure information remains current.
*   **KYC Risk Rating:** As part of the KYC process, customers are assigned a risk rating, which can be either "standard" or "enhanced." This rating determines the level of subsequent monitoring scrutiny applied to the customer. It is crucial to distinguish this from a Credit Risk Score, which measures borrower default risk rather than financial-crime risk.
*   **Active Monitoring:** The established KYC profile is used for active monitoring of transactions. This involves comparing the actual flow of funds against the customer's profile to identify and flag any unusual activities, such as unexpected transaction volumes or structuring.

## Aliases

Customer Due Diligence is also known by the following terms:
*   cdd
*   kyc
*   know-your-customer
