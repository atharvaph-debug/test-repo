# Customer Lifetime Value Overview

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), also known as LTV or customer LTV, represents the total expected net profit earned from a customer over the entire duration of their relationship. This metric is crucial for understanding the long-term value a customer brings to the business.

## Definition and Calculation

CLV is defined as the total expected net profit earned from a customer over their entire relationship. It is calculated as projected revenue minus the cost to serve and retain the customer across their lifetime.

## Semantic Mapping

While "LTV" or "customer LTV" are frequently used by marketing departments, and "CLV" is often used in finance reporting, these terms refer to the identical metric. For metadata enrichment purposes, they must be reconciled under a single metadata definition to ensure consistent understanding and usage across different departments.

## Usage

Customer Lifetime Value is primarily used to justify customer acquisition and retention spend. It plays a strategic role in activities such as targeting retention offers and tracking the efficiency of retention efforts, particularly when analyzed in conjunction with customer churn data.
