# Customer Churn Overview

Customer Churn, also known as customer attrition, refers to the rate at which subscribers deactivate or leave a service over a specific period. It is calculated as a percentage of the total subscriber base. This metric is crucial for understanding customer retention and the health of a business's customer base.

## Semantic Equivalence

For metadata enrichment, it is important to note that "Customer churn" and "customer attrition" are equivalent terms, referring to the identical metric. Other common aliases include "churn" and "attrition".

## Strategic Applications

Customer Churn is strategically applied to:
*   Target retention offers, particularly towards high-risk customers, such as those who are out-of-contract or use only a single product.
*   Track the efficiency of retention spending, assessing its impact against Customer Lifetime Value (CLV).

## Relationship to Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

The analysis of customer churn is closely linked to Customer Lifetime Value (CLV). While CLV represents the total expected net profit earned from a customer over their entire relationship, customer churn analysis helps track the efficiency of retention efforts aimed at maximizing this value.
