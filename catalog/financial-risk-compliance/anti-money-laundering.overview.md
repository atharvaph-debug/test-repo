# Anti-Money Laundering Overview

Anti-Money Laundering (AML) refers to risk-based transaction and behavioral monitoring programs designed to detect deviations from established customer baselines and identify suspicious activities. These programs are critical components of an organization's financial crime risk and compliance framework, focusing specifically on managing financial-crime risk.

## Role in Financial Crime Risk & Compliance

AML is an integral part of the Compliance Lifecycle Pipeline, following the initial Know Your Customer (KYC) rating and preceding the filing of a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR). It involves ongoing risk-based transaction monitoring that leverages the customer's KYC risk profile—established during onboarding (via standard or enhanced due diligence) and based on identity and beneficial ownership verification—as a crucial metadata baseline.

The primary objective of AML monitoring is to detect deviations from this established profile, such as unexpected transaction volumes or structuring patterns, that could indicate money laundering activities. This process maintains a critical metadata distinction, focusing on a customer's financial-crime risk, which should not be conflated with their credit risk score (measuring likelihood of default).

When an investigation stemming from AML monitoring confirms reasonable suspicion, it leads to the formal output of a SAR, which is subject to strict regulatory timelines and a "no tipping off" policy.
