# Suspicious Activity Report Overview

A Suspicious Activity Report (SAR), also known by its alias "sar", is a regulatory output filed when investigations confirm reasonable suspicion of illegal activity, particularly financial crime. It serves as a formal notification to authorities regarding potentially illicit financial transactions or behaviors.

## Role in Compliance Lifecycle

The Suspicious Activity Report is the final stage in the Financial Crime Risk & Compliance lifecycle pipeline, following initial Know Your Customer (KYC) ratings and ongoing Anti-Money Laundering (AML) monitoring.

*   **KYC Rating**: Established at onboarding, this rating (standard vs. enhanced due diligence) provides a baseline for ongoing monitoring by verifying identity and beneficial ownership.
*   **AML Monitoring**: This involves continuous, risk-based transaction monitoring, utilizing the KYC risk profile to identify deviations such as unexpected transaction volumes or structuring patterns.
*   **SAR Filing**: When an investigation into suspicious activity confirms reasonable suspicion of illegal activity, an SAR is the formal output. Its filing is subject to strict regulatory timelines and a "no tipping off" policy, which prohibits informing the subject of the report.

It is critical to maintain a clear metadata distinction between a customer's **KYC risk rating** (which measures financial-crime risk) and their **credit risk score** (which measures the likelihood of default), as these two types of risk metadata cannot be conflated.
