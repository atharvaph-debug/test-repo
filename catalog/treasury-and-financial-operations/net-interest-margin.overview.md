# Net Interest Margin Overview

Net Interest Margin (NIM) is a core profitability metric in treasury and financial operations, serving as an important index in earnings and treasury reports. It is also known by its alias, `nim`.

## Definition and Calculation

Net Interest Margin is calculated as net interest income divided by average interest-earning assets. Specifically, it is derived from the difference between Interest Income and Interest Expense, then divided by Average Earning Assets, as shown in the formula:

$$\text{NIM} = \frac{\text{Interest Income} - \text{Interest Expense}}{\text{Average Earning Assets}}$$

This calculation provides the core metric for balance-sheet profitability.

## Business Role and Market Dynamics

NIM serves as a key profitability index within an organization's financial reporting. Treasury departments actively monitor NIM in relation to the prevailing rate environment. Compression in Net Interest Margin can occur when the costs of funding rise at a faster rate than the yields generated by interest-earning assets.
