# Liquidity Coverage Ratio Overview

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is a critical Basel III regulatory ratio designed to ensure financial institutions maintain sufficient high-quality liquid assets to cover net cash outflows under a stressed scenario. It is defined as high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) divided by total 30-day net cash outflows under stress.

## Key Features

### Definition and Calculation
The LCR is calculated as the ratio of High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to Total Net Cash Outflows over a 30-calendar-day stress scenario. This ratio is governed by Basel III regulatory standards to promote short-term resilience of banks' liquidity risk profiles.

The formula is:
$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA)}}{\text{Total Net Cash Outflows over a 30-calendar-day stress scenario}}$

### Regulatory and Internal Thresholds
Compliance with LCR regulations is mandatory, with an absolute regulatory floor set at $\ge 100\%$. Beyond regulatory compliance, the institution maintains an internal management target buffer of approximately $\mathbf{110\%}$. This internal target serves as a proactive measure, triggering internal escalations if the LCR approaches appetite limits, thereby preventing potential breaches of the regulatory threshold.

### Operational Management
Treasury teams actively manage the LCR, forecasting it daily and adjusting the HQLA portfolio as needed to maintain the internal management target. Any projected movement of the LCR toward internal appetite limits necessitates immediate escalation.
