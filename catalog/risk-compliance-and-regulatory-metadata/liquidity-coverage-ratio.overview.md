# Liquidity Coverage Ratio Overview

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is a key Basel III short-term liquidity standard designed to ensure that banks maintain sufficient High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to cover total net cash outflows over a 30-day stress scenario. It serves as a primary metric for maintaining organizational risk within acceptable boundaries.

## Key Features

*   **Definition**: The LCR measures whether a bank holds sufficient High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to survive a 30-day stress scenario, comparing these assets against total net cash outflows expected during that period.
*   **Formula**: The Liquidity Coverage Ratio is calculated using the following formula:
    $$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{HQLA}}{\text{Total Net Cash Outflows over 30 Days}}$$
*   **Regulatory Standard**: A regulatory binding floor for the LCR is strictly **100%**. This figure governs over any conflicting internal documentation, as mandated by the Basel III Regulatory Filing.
*   **Risk Management Role**: The LCR is an essential metric, alongside credit risk scores, used in risk management frameworks to control and monitor risk.
