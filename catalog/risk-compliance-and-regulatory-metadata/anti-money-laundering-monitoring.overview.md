# Anti-Money Laundering Monitoring Overview

Anti-Money Laundering (AML) monitoring, also known by its aliases `aml` and `aml-monitoring`, is a risk-based framework designed to evaluate customer activity deviations against baseline profiles established during the Know Your Customer (KYC) process. It serves as a crucial component of financial crime controls within an organization.

## Methodology

AML monitoring operates on a risk-based framework. Its primary function is to evaluate real-world customer activity for deviations such as unusual transaction volumes, structuring patterns, or anomalous fund movements. These observed activities are then compared against baseline profiles that were established during the Know Your Customer (KYC) onboarding process ([AML Investigations Playbook](https://atharva-test-izfs7ihz.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/KCD/pages/8683541)).

## Context in Financial Crime Compliance

AML monitoring is one of the three core operational pillars of financial-crime controls, alongside Know Your Customer (KYC) and Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR). These three pillars provide a baseline for ensuring compliance and mitigating financial crime risks ([Compliance Policy Handbook](https://atharva-test-izfs7ihz.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/KCD/pages/8454166)). KYC, in particular, involves verifying a customer's identity and assessing their associated risk, being initiated at onboarding and refreshed periodically ([Compliance Policy Handbook](https://atharva-test-izfs7ihz.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/KCD/pages/8454166)).
