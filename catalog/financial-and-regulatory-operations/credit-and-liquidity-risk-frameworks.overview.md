# Credit and Liquidity Risk Frameworks Overview

Credit and Liquidity Risk Frameworks encompass the methodologies used to calculate borrower-level default risk and track institutional exposure, crucial for treasury and credit risk mitigation. These frameworks provide structured approaches to quantify and manage financial risks.

## Key Features

### Credit Risk Framework
Credit risk, defined as the risk of a borrower defaulting on their obligations, is quantified through a **credit risk score**. This score is calculated using several key factors:
*   **Repayment history**: Past performance of the borrower regarding debt repayment.
*   **Current exposure**: The existing financial commitment the institution has to the borrower.
*   **Income/leverage**: The borrower's financial capacity and debt burden.

Credit risk scores serve dual purposes:
*   **At origination**: To determine and appropriately price new lending products.
*   **Ongoing monitoring**: To continuously assess the health of the overall loan portfolio.

These credit risk metrics are managed collaboratively across Treasury and Finance departments.

### Liquidity Risk Framework (Basel III & LCR)
The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is a core component of the Basel III framework, setting the standard for short-term liquidity management. The LCR requires banks to maintain a sufficient buffer of high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to cover their total net cash outflows during a severe 30-calendar-day stress scenario. The regulatory minimum for the LCR must be at least 100%.

The LCR is calculated using the following formula:

`LCR = HQLA / Total Net Cash Outflows over 30 Days`
