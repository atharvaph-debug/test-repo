# Liquidity Coverage Ratio Overview

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is a critical Basel III standard designed to ensure financial institutions maintain sufficient high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to cover anticipated net outflows during a 30-calendar-day stress scenario. It serves as the standard for short-term liquidity within the Basel III framework.

## Key Features

*   **Definition**: The LCR metadata definition in Group Treasury systems represents high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) divided by 30-day net cash outflows.
*   **Purpose**: It requires banks to hold enough HQLA to cover total net cash outflows over a 30-calendar-day stress scenario.
*   **Regulatory Standard**: As part of Basel III, it has a regulatory minimum requirement where the ratio must be at least 100%.
*   **Internal Management**: Financial institutions, particularly Group Treasury systems, manage LCR against internal targets, often maintaining a safety headroom above regulatory minimums. For example, systems might manage this against an internal target of approximately 110%.

## Formula

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio is calculated as follows:

```
LCR = HQLA / Total Net Cash Outflows over 30 Days
```
