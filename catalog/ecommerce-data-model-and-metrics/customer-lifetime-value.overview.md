# Customer Lifetime Value Overview

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), also known as CLV, LTV, or customer LTV, represents the projected net profit generated by a customer throughout their entire relationship timeline with a business. It is a key metric in eCommerce Data Model & Metrics for understanding the long-term value of customers.

## Definition and Formula

CLV quantifies the net profit expected from a customer across their entire relationship lifecycle. It is formulated as:

$$\text{CLV} = \text{Projected Customer Revenue} - (\text{Cost to Serve} + \text{Cost to Retain})$$

## Purpose and Usage

This metric is crucial for justifying budget allocations for customer acquisition and retention efforts, helping to direct investment towards high-value customer segments.

## Synonym Standardization

For consistent reporting, especially between departments like Marketing (which commonly uses "LTV") and Finance (which typically uses "CLV"), it is essential to reconcile these terms to prevent double-counting or disjointed reporting. All three terms — CLV, LTV, and customer LTV — refer to the exact same metric.
