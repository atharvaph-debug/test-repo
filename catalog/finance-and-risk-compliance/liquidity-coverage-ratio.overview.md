# Liquidity Coverage Ratio Overview

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR), also known by its alias LCR, is a critical finance and risk compliance metric defined as the ratio of high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to projected net cash outflows over a 30-day stress period. It is managed under Basel III regulatory standards and serves as a core measure of a financial institution's short-term resilience against liquidity shocks.

## Key Features

The LCR is calculated based on two primary components:
*   **High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA):** These are assets that can be easily and immediately converted into cash at little or no loss of value, forming the numerator of the ratio.
*   **30-day Net Cash Outflows:** This represents the total expected cash outflows minus total expected cash inflows over a prospective 30-calendar-day period, calculated under an acute stress scenario, forming the denominator.

**Regulatory Context and Target:** The management of the LCR is guided by Basel III regulatory filings. While there is a regulatory minimum, the internal management target is maintained at approximately 110%, ensuring a buffer above the minimum requirement. Breaches of this internal target trigger an escalation process before regulatory minimums are reached.

**Operational Management:** The LCR is operationally managed daily by the Group Treasury, primarily through adjustments to the HQLA portfolio to maintain the target ratio.
