# Liquidity Coverage Ratio Rules Overview

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is a critical regulatory and treasury metric defined under Basel III regulations, designed to ensure that banks maintain sufficient high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to cover potential net cash outflows during a 30-calendar-day stress scenario. As a fundamental piece of financial and risk metadata, LCR serves as a key indicator of an institution's short-term liquidity resilience.

## Definition and Calculation

The LCR is calculated as the ratio of High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to total net cash outflows over a 30-day period:

$$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{HQLA}}{\text{Total Net Cash Outflows over 30 Days}}$$

*   **High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA):** These assets are categorized into Level 1 and Level 2 tiers, and mandated regulatory haircuts are applied to their values.
*   **Total Net Cash Outflows:** These are modeled by applying standardized run-off rates to liabilities and by capping inflows on receivables.

LCR forecasts are updated daily to reflect current conditions and projections.

## Regulatory Compliance and Monitoring

The regulatory minimum floor for LCR is **100%**. While internal management targets may exceed this (e.g., maintaining headroom at approximately 110% as an internal risk-appetite decision), any breach of the 100% floor, even intraday on reporting dates, constitutes a mandatory notifiable event. Such a breach requires immediate escalation to Treasury and Regulatory Reporting leads. The LCR is explicitly identified as a "regulatory liquidity metric" within the broader category of "Financial & Risk Metadata," highlighting its importance for compliance and risk management.
