# Anti-Money Laundering Overview

Anti-Money Laundering (AML) refers to broad transaction monitoring designed to identify patterns inconsistent with expected behavior and support suspicious activity reporting. It is a critical component of risk, compliance, and security frameworks.

## Definition and Purpose
AML is the process of monitoring transactions to identify patterns that are inconsistent with expected customer behavior. While Know Your Customer (KYC) processes verify *who* the customer is, AML monitors *what* the customer does, ensuring compliance with regulations designed to prevent illicit financial activities.

## Operational Context
The Anti-Money Laundering process is closely tied to Know Your Customer (KYC) procedures. The customer risk rating established during KYC (which can be Standard or Enhanced) serves as a foundational parameter for downstream AML monitoring activities. When AML monitoring reveals reasonable suspicion of illicit conduct, it leads to the confidential regulatory filing of a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR).

## Metadata Dependencies
The relationship between these compliance activities forms a clear metadata dependency chain:
KYC Risk Rating $\rightarrow$ AML Monitoring $\rightarrow$ SAR (Final Output)

This sequence illustrates how the initial customer assessment (KYC Risk Rating) directly informs and influences the ongoing transaction surveillance (AML Monitoring), which can ultimately result in a formal regulatory disclosure (SAR).
