# Number Portability Overview

Number Portability, also known as Local Number Portability (LNP), Mobile Number Portability (MNP), or "porting," refers to the regulator-mandated capability that enables subscribers to retain their existing telephone numbers when they change carriers. This capability involves a complex metadata exchange, verification rules, and specific processes to facilitate the transfer of numbers.

## Core Definitions and Concepts

The overarching concept of **Number Portability** ensures that telephone subscribers can keep their phone numbers even if they switch service providers. This is a regulator-mandated capability aimed at promoting competition and consumer choice within the telecommunications industry.

## Key Actors in Portability

The process of number portability involves several key entities:
*   **Recipient Carrier:** This is the gaining carrier, which is the new service provider receiving the ported telephone number.
*   **Donor Carrier:** This is the losing carrier, the original service provider that releases the number to the new carrier.
*   **Central Porting Clearinghouse:** This entity acts as a centralized registry responsible for routing portability requests between carriers.

## Metadata Exchange and Validation Rules

The successful execution of number portability relies heavily on accurate metadata exchange and stringent validation rules. This sub-topic specifically focuses on these aspects:
*   **Information Matching:** Port-in requests require the precise collection of essential subscriber metadata, including the phone number being ported, the current account identifier with the donor carrier, and any donor-specific Personal Identification Numbers (PINs). Discrepancies in these details are a primary cause of rejections during the donor's validation period.
*   **Regulatory Compliance:** Regulatory frameworks dictate maximum timelines for the validation window during a porting request. While donor carriers are permitted to offer retention incentives during a port-out request, they are explicitly prohibited from delaying or blocking a valid port-out request.
