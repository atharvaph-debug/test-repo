# Net Interest Margin Overview

Net Interest Margin (NIM), also known by the alias `nim`, is a key balance-sheet profitability metric primarily used within Treasury and Capital Markets. It is calculated as net interest income divided by average interest-earning assets.

## Definition and Calculation

Net Interest Margin is a core operational metric that Group Treasury uses to track balance-sheet profitability. The formula for NIM is:

$$\text{NIM} = \frac{\text{Interest Income} - \text{Interest Expense}}{\text{Average Earning Assets}}$$

This formula represents the net interest income (Interest Income minus Interest Expense) divided by the Average Earning Assets.

## Sensitivity

Treasury models show that NIM can compress when market-wide funding costs rise faster than asset yields, which can impact overall balance-sheet liquidity.

## Context in Financial Metrics

NIM is one of several financial metrics employed, distinct from measures like the Credit Risk Score, which estimates borrower default risk based on factors such as repayment history and income. The Credit Risk Score, in turn, is structurally distinct from the KYC risk rating, which specifically measures financial-crime risk and does not calculate default risk.
