# Customer Lifetime Value Overview

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), also known as LTV or Customer LTV, is a key financial and analytical metric that quantifies the net long-term profit projected from a customer over the entire duration of their relationship with the business. It is a standard metric used in corporate and analytical applications to understand the financial worth of customers.

## Definition and Formula

CLV represents the total net profit expected from a customer throughout their engagement. It is calculated using the following formula:

$$\text{CLV} = \text{Projected Customer Revenue} - (\text{Cost to Serve} + \text{Cost to Retain})$$

## Business Application

This metric is crucial for justifying budgets related to customer acquisition and retention. By segmenting users based on their CLV, businesses can ensure that high-value customer cohorts receive proportionally higher investments, optimizing resource allocation [[Finance Business Glossary](https://atharva-test-izfs7ihz.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/KCD/pages/8486916)].

## Semantic Alignment

While marketing materials frequently use terms like "customer LTV" or "LTV," official Finance reporting has standardized on "CLV." Analytics pipelines are designed to map these synonyms to the single, consistent metric of Customer Lifetime Value to prevent potential dual-definition errors and ensure data accuracy [[Finance Business Glossary](https://atharva-test-izfs7ihz.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/KCD/pages/8486916)].
