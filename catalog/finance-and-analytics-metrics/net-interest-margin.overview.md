# Net Interest Margin Overview

Net Interest Margin (NIM) is a core corporate profitability metric that measures the net yield generated by a firm's balance sheet per dollar of earning assets. It tracks the net yield generated by earning assets relative to their average value, providing insight into the efficiency of a financial institution's interest-earning activities.

## Definition and Calculation

NIM is calculated by dividing the Net Interest Income by the Average Interest-Earning Assets. This metric quantifies the net yield generated by a firm's balance sheet per dollar of earning assets.

The formula for Net Interest Margin is:

$$\text{NIM} = \frac{\text{Net Interest Income}}{\text{Average Interest-Earning Assets}}$$

## Reporting and Fluctuations

NIM is primarily reported in corporate earnings and treasury reviews. Its value is known to fluctuate significantly with changes in the prevailing interest-rate environment.

## Aliases

This metric is also commonly referred to as NIM.
