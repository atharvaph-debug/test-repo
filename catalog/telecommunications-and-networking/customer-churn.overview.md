# Customer Churn Overview

Customer Churn, also known as customer attrition, refers to the percentage of a subscriber base that deactivates during a given quarter. This metric is a key indicator in telecommunications, reflecting subscriber retention and satisfaction.

## Definition and Synonyms

"Customer churn" and "customer attrition" are synonymous terms used to describe subscribers who voluntarily depart from a service. It specifically measures the percentage of the subscriber base that deactivates within a quarterly period.

## Churn Drivers

Voluntary customer churn is primarily influenced by several factors:
*   **Price:** This is particularly concentrated among out-of-contract subscribers.
*   **Network Quality:** Poor coverage and a high incidence of dropped calls contribute significantly to churn.
*   **Competitor Offers:** Attractive incentives, such as port-in offers from competitors, can lead subscribers to switch services.

Data indicates that subscribers who use only a single product churn at a substantially higher rate compared to those who subscribe to multiple services.

## Retention Strategies

To mitigate churn, strategies include frontline agents utilizing approved retention offers for out-of-contract, single-product subscribers before competitor offers become active. Additionally, resolving network latency issues and reducing dropped calls are direct measures to decrease quality-driven churn.
