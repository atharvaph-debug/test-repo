# Number Portability Overview

Number Portability, also known as Local Number Portability (LNP), Mobile Number Portability (MNP), or simply "porting," is the regulatory-mandated process that enables subscribers to retain their existing telephone number when switching between telecommunications carriers. This capability allows subscribers to keep their number regardless of their service provider.

## Key Features and Processes

The number portability process involves three main entities: a *Recipient (gaining) carrier*, a *Donor (losing) carrier*, and a *central porting clearinghouse*.

### Port-In Process
When a subscriber initiates a "port-in" request, the Recipient carrier collects the subscriber's current account details and PINs, then submits this request to the central clearinghouse. The most common cause of rejections during the donor validation window are mismatched details in the request.

### Port-Out Process
During a "port-out," the Donor carrier is required to validate the request and release the numbers within strict regulatory timelines. While carriers cannot block valid port-outs, they may present a retention offer to the subscriber before releasing the number as part of their strategy to reduce churn. Voluntary departures, such as port-outs, are a significant driver of churn, which is defined as the percentage of the subscriber base that deactivates during a given period.

## Metadata Enrichment Considerations

For telecom-related data enrichment, metadata schemas must preserve the functional distinction between subscriber identity and network access paths. This is critical to prevent reporting and routing errors within the system, ensuring that number portability processes are accurately tracked and managed without confusing the subscriber's enduring identity with the changing network infrastructure they connect through.
