# Customer Due Diligence Overview

Customer Due Diligence (CDD) defines the verification policies and financial-crime checks applied to customers during onboarding and account lifecycle monitoring. It is an essential component of Financial Crime Compliance (FCC) and is frequently used interchangeably with Know Your Customer (KYC).

## Key Concepts

CDD, often referred to as KYC, mandates the verification of customer identity, which includes identifying beneficial owners for legal entities. This process leads to the assignment of either a standard or enhanced risk profile to customers, which quantifies financial-crime and money-laundering risk. This KYC Risk Rating is distinct from other customer profile metrics.

## Related Concepts

*   **KYC Risk Rating**: This metric quantifies the financial-crime and money-laundering risk associated with a customer, leading to designations like *Standard* versus *Enhanced* due diligence levels. It is a key output of the Customer Due Diligence process and is stored on the Customer Profile.
*   **Credit Risk Score**: Unlike the KYC Risk Rating, the Credit Risk Score quantifies the financial probability of loan or product default. It is calculated from factors such as historical repayment patterns, existing credit exposures, and customer leverage ratios. Data platforms must distinguish between these two ratings to ensure high data quality within downstream analytics, as they serve different purposes: KYC for financial crime vulnerability, and Credit Risk for default probability.
*   **Anti-Money Laundering (AML)**: CDD establishes the baseline customer profile which is then used by AML efforts. AML involves ongoing system and behavioral monitoring designed to spot transactions inconsistent with a customer's expected, established KYC profile.
*   **Suspicious Activity Report (SAR)**: When internal transaction monitoring and investigator review establish a reasonable suspicion of financial crime, a formal, confidential SAR is compiled and sent to regulatory agencies.
