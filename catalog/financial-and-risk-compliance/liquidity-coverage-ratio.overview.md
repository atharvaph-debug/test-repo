# Liquidity Coverage Ratio Overview

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR), also known by its aliases LCR and Basel III Liquidity Standards, is a fundamental financial metric within the Financial & Risk Compliance category. It is an essential short-term liquidity standard defined under the Basel III regulatory framework. This ratio measures whether a financial institution holds sufficient High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to cover its projected net cash outflows over a 30-day stress period.

## Key Concepts

The LCR ensures that institutions can withstand significant liquidity shocks, specifically a 30-day stress scenario, by maintaining an adequate buffer of easily monetizable assets. It is a critical tool for managing liquidity risk.

## Calculation

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio is calculated as the ratio of High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to Total Net Cash Outflows over a 30-day period. The regulatory requirement dictates that this ratio must be at least 100%.

$$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{HQLA}}{\text{Total Net Cash Outflows over 30 Days}} \ge 100\%$$

Here, `HQLA` refers to High-Quality Liquid Assets, and `Total Net Cash Outflows over 30 Days` represents the anticipated cash outflows during a specified stress scenario within a 30-day timeframe.

## Regulatory Requirements and Targets

The LCR must never fall below the 100% regulatory minimum. This minimum applies even intraday on filing dates. Any breach of this 100% threshold is considered a legally notifiable event, triggering an immediate escalation to Treasury and Regulatory Reporting leadership. To proactively prevent breaches of the Basel III regulatory minimums, the organization has set an internal target for the LCR of 110%.

## Role in Risk Management

The LCR plays a crucial role in liquidity risk control by ensuring that institutions maintain robust liquidity buffers. It is the primary mechanism through which liquidity risk is managed, forming a cornerstone of the risk management framework.
