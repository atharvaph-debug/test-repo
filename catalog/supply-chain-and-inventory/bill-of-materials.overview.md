# Bill of Materials Overview

A Bill of Materials (BOM) is an authoritative, structured engineering specification that details every component, sub-assembly, and the precise quantity required to manufacture one unit of a finished-good SKU. It serves as a fundamental "structural recipe" for assembled or manufactured items.

## Definition and Purpose

The Bill of Materials is defined as a structured engineering specification ([Bill of Materials — Engineering Specification](https://atharva-test-izfs7ihz.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/KCD/pages/8355883)). For each manufactured or assembled finished-good SKU, a BOM is bound to it, cataloging the exact components, sub-assemblies, and quantities needed to build a single unit of that SKU ([Inventory Systems Overview](https://atharva-test-izfs7ihz.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/KCD/pages/8978433)).

## Integration and Usage

The BOM acts as a critical bridge between engineering intent and procurement operations. Any modification made to the BOM will automatically adjust the procurement shopping list ([Bill of Materials — Engineering Specification](https://atharva-test-izfs7ihz.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/KCD/pages/8355883)). It is also essential for automated requisition processes: when a SKU's physical inventory falls to its reorder point, the planning system generates a purchase requisition. For manufactured goods, this requisition is "exploded" against its BOM, ensuring that component raw materials are ordered in corresponding, proportional quantities ([Procurement Standard Operating Procedure](https://atharva-test-izfs7ihz.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/KCD/pages/9043969)).
