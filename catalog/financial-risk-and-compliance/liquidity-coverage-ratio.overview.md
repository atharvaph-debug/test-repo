# Liquidity Coverage Ratio Overview

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is a critical Basel III regulatory standard designed to measure a bank's short-term liquidity stability. Managed by risk, treasury, and finance leadership, it ensures that banks maintain sufficient High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to cover potential net cash outflows during a 30-day stress scenario.

## Key Features

*   **Definition & Standard**: The LCR is a Basel III short-term liquidity standard. Banks are required to maintain a regulatory minimum of at least 100% to ensure short-term stability.
*   **Formula & Calculation**: The LCR is calculated as the ratio of High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to Total Net Cash Outflows over a 30-day stress scenario:
    $$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA)}}{\text{Total Net Cash Outflows over a 30-day stress scenario}}$$
*   **Interpretation**: A ratio of exactly 100% signifies that a bank's HQLA is precisely enough to cover its modeled 30-day stressed outflows. Operational guidelines mandate that the LCR must never drop below this 100% threshold.
