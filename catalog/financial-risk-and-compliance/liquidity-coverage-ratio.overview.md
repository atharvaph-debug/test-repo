# Liquidity Coverage Ratio Overview

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is a critical short-term liquidity standard under Basel III guidelines, designed to ensure financial institutions maintain sufficient high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to cover projected net cash outflows over a 30-calendar-day stressed period. It is a key regulatory metric used to manage and monitor liquidity risk.

## Calculation

The LCR is calculated as the ratio of High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to total net cash outflows over a 30-calendar-day stressed period.

$$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA)}}{\text{Projected Net Cash Outflows over a 30-day stress period}}$$

HQLA represents assets that can be easily and immediately converted into cash at little or no loss of value, while the denominator accounts for potential cash outflows during a period of significant financial stress.

## Regulatory and Internal Targets

The regulatory minimum LCR requirement is **100%** as stipulated by Basel III parameters. Internally, the management target is maintained at approximately **110%**. This internal target provides comfortable headroom above the regulatory minimum and helps ensure regulatory compliance.

## Compliance and Escalation

Any breach of the 100% regulatory minimum, including intraday on a reporting date, is considered a critical notifiable event that requires immediate escalation to Treasury and Regulatory Reporting leadership. Breaches of the internal management target of 110% also trigger immediate internal escalation, serving as an early warning prior to potentially violating regulatory minimums.
