# Customer Lifetime Value Overview

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) is a crucial financial metric representing the total net profit expected from a customer over their entire relationship. This metric, also known by aliases such as CLV, LTV, and customer LTV, is fundamental for strategic decision-making in marketing and finance.

## Aliases and Semantic Alignment

The terms "Customer Lifetime Value", "CLV", "customer LTV", and "LTV" all refer to the exact same metric and should be treated as semantically identical data fields.

## Definition

CLV is defined as the total net profit expected from a customer over their entire relationship with a business. This projection is derived by subtracting the cost to serve and retain the customer from their projected revenue.

## Use Case

This metric primarily drives decisions related to customer acquisition and retention spend limits. Different departments may refer to it using various aliases; for example, "LTV" or "customer LTV" are commonly used in marketing decks, while "CLV" is typically found in Finance reports.
