# Liquidity Coverage Ratio Overview

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is a primary short-term liquidity risk control metric. It quantifies a financial institution's ability to withstand a significant stress scenario by measuring its high-quality liquid assets against potential net cash outflows over a 30-day period.

## Definition and Formula

The LCR is defined by the ratio of an institution's High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to its total net cash outflows over a 30-day stress scenario. The formula for LCR is:

$$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA)}}{\text{Total Net Cash Outflows over 30-day stress scenario}}$$

## Regulatory Thresholds and Targets

Under Basel III rules, a binding regulatory floor for the LCR is set at exactly 100%. Any breach of this minimum, even intraday on a reporting date, is considered a notifiable event requiring immediate escalation to Regulatory Reporting and Treasury leads.

To proactively manage this regulatory limit and accommodate normal fluctuations, an internal management target for the LCR is maintained at 110%.
